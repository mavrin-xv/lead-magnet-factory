# Twelve laws for a lead magnet people finish

1. **One binding idea.** One transformation, one promise, one reader. "AI, automation and content marketing" is three magnets.
2. **The title is the outcome.** Number or time + result + reader. The format word ("playbook") is not the promise. See `magnet-concept/references/title-formula.md`.
3. **Hook fact first.** Open with one number that makes the reader feel their problem, with a source. "Most cafes" is not a number. If there is no sourced number, use a concrete first-hand observation instead and say it is first-hand.
4. **Quick win in the first 20 to 25%.** The reader should get a result from the first section alone and think "I can use this today" before the middle.
5. **Swipe and deploy.** Every action item can be copied or done as written. "Consider improving your emails" fails; the email itself, ready to send, passes.
6. **Show, do not tell.** A real or realistic example beats abstract advice every time. Each step has one.
7. **Give most of it away.** The magnet should be worth paying for. If you worry you gave too much, you probably gave the right amount. The offer sells implementation, speed or the next problem, not the missing half.
8. **Numbers over adjectives.** "Cuts it from 4 hours to 25 minutes" beats "saves a lot of time". Only numbers you can source.
9. **Scannable.** No paragraph over 3 sentences. Headings, short lists, one idea per block. A reader skimming the headings alone should get the outline of the method.
10. **The reader's words.** Write in the terms the reader uses about their own problem, taken from their messages, reviews and survey answers. Beginners get no jargon; an expert audience gets their own shorthand, not yours.
11. **A next step that is earned.** Structure the end as: what you just got, what happens if you use it, the one next step. It reads as the natural continuation, not a pitch bolted on.
12. **One call to action.** One action, one destination: a keyword to comment or reply, or one link. Never a menu.

## Anti-patterns to cut on sight

- An intro that explains why the topic matters for three paragraphs before any value.
- "In this guide you will learn..." followed by the table of contents in prose.
- Advice that applies to everyone ("be consistent", "know your audience").
- A filled example that still has `[YOUR NAME]` in it.
- A closing section with four links.
- Fake urgency ("only today") in an evergreen magnet.
