# The outcome title formula

A lead magnet title names the result, not the process or the format.

```
[number or time] + [outcome the reader wants] + [for whom / in what situation]
```

The format word is optional and goes last if at all. "Playbook", "guide" and "ebook" in front of the title describe your work, not the reader's result.

## Examples (invented businesses)

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| The Ultimate Espresso Guide | Dial in your espresso in 15 minutes: 22 checks for small cafes |
| Wholesale Coffee Playbook | Your first wholesale order without overstock: a 10-minute calculator for 1 to 3 person cafes |
| Email Marketing Tips | 5 emails that bring back a lapsed regular within 2 weeks |
| Bakery Pricing Template | Price a new pastry in 20 minutes so it pays for its own labour |

## Rules

1. One outcome. "Grow and save time and..." is two magnets.
2. A number, a time or a unit (minutes, steps, emails, days). It makes the promise checkable.
3. The reader's words. If the reader says "my shots taste different", do not title it "extraction consistency".
4. No hype words: ultimate, secret, hack, unlock, skyrocket, game-changer.
5. Under about 70 characters, so it fits a social card and an email subject line.
6. If the language of the audience builds titles differently (word order, verb forms), keep the parts and drop the English word order. The parts are what matter: outcome, unit, reader.

## A question-style variant

Some audiences respond better to a "How to..." title. Keep the same parts: "How to dial in espresso in 15 minutes without wasting a bag". The outcome and the number stay; only the frame changes.

## Check

Read the title alone, without the page. Can the reader tell (1) what they will have at the end, (2) roughly how long it takes, (3) whether it is for them? Three yeses or rewrite.
