# The five tests

Score every concept 1 to 5 on each test before anything is written. A score below 3 on any single test rejects the concept, however good the others are. Bridge and Value count double in the total, because a magnet that leads nowhere, or is not worth taking, fails the business job even when it reads well.

## 1. Specific (one reader, one problem, one result)

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | "A guide to marketing" |
| 3 | "Email marketing for cafes" |
| 5 | "5 emails that bring a lapsed regular back within 2 weeks, for cafes with a loyalty list" |

Ask: could two very different people both feel this was written for them? If yes, it is not specific yet.

## 2. Bridge (does it lead to the paid offer)

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | The magnet's topic has nothing to do with what is sold |
| 3 | Same broad topic, but the reader could finish it and never need the offer |
| 5 | Finishing the magnet surfaces the exact problem the offer solves, and says so |

Ask: after the quick win, what is the reader's next problem? Is the offer the answer to it?

## 3. Quick win (a result in under 30 minutes)

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Reading only; the result comes "over time" |
| 3 | A result in an afternoon, with some setup |
| 5 | A visible result in under 15 minutes with what the reader already has |

Write the quick win as a sentence with a verb and an object: "pulls one shot that tastes the same as yesterday's".

## 4. Value (would someone pay for this)

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Available in the first search result, in the same depth |
| 3 | Useful, but the reader would not miss it |
| 5 | The reader would pay a small amount for it, and some would say it was worth more |

Think in four parts (from Hormozi's value equation): the size of the outcome, how likely the reader believes it will work for them, how fast it works, how little effort it takes. Raise the last two first: they are the cheapest to improve.

## 5. AI-proof (does it beat a free chatbot answer)

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | A chatbot gives the same thing in one prompt |
| 3 | Has some first-hand detail, but mostly general advice |
| 5 | Built on things only the owner has: their numbers, their mistakes, their templates, their customers' words, a tool that computes something for the reader |

Ask: what is in here that a model trained on the public web could not write? If the answer is "nothing", add owner data or turn it into a tool.

## Reasons to drop a concept outright

- The quick win needs a purchase, an account or a tool the reader does not have.
- It is a "complete guide" or "ultimate" anything. Big magnets do not get finished, and unfinished magnets do not convert.
- The promise needs a number nobody can source.
